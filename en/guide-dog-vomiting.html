Dog Vomit Colors and When to Call the Vet

The bottom line first: one episode of vomiting in a dog who otherwise seems fine can be watched — but blood in the vomit, repeated vomiting, or unproductive retching with nothing coming up means go to the vet right away.

First, is it vomiting or regurgitation?

"My dog threw up" can mean two very different things. Vomiting involves clear abdominal effort — heaving and retching — and is usually preceded by nausea signs such as drooling or lip-licking. What comes up has been at least partly worked on by the stomach. Regurgitation is far more passive: food simply slides back out with little effort, usually right after eating or drinking, and often still looks like intact kibble, sometimes in a tube shape. Repeated regurgitation points to the esophagus rather than the stomach. When you call the clinic, describing how long after eating it happened and whether your dog strained is genuinely useful information for the diagnosis.

Reading the color and contents

Color narrows down the possibilities — it is a hint, not a diagnosis. Still, it is worth knowing which colors sit closer to the danger end.

  • Yellow or greenish (bile) — most often bile coming up and irritating an empty stomach. Early-morning vomiting on an empty stomach usually falls into this group. Occasional episodes are frequently harmless, but daily ones deserve a check.
  • White foam or clear liquid — can be stomach acid, saliva, or the result of gulping down too much water at once. However, repeated white foam along with a swollen or painful belly can indicate an emergency such as bloat/GDV — go in immediately.
  • Undigested food — often eating too fast, overeating, or regurgitation. See whether a slow-feeder bowl and smaller meals fix it.
  • Brown or coffee-ground looking — could be digested kibble, but it can also be digested blood from the stomach or small intestine. Don't brush this one off; call your vet.
  • Red blood in the vomit — treat fresh blood in vomit as an emergency and go to the vet.
  • Grass, plastic, string or other foreign material — if there's any chance your dog swallowed an object, time matters. Note what, when, and how much, and tell the clinic.

Go straight to the vet if you see any of these

  • Blood in the vomit, or a coffee-ground appearance
  • Repeated vomiting within a few hours, or vomiting that continues past a day
  • Retching with nothing coming up, especially with a distended belly or restlessness (emergency)
  • Any chance your dog ate something toxic — chocolate, grapes, onion, xylitol — or swallowed an object
  • Vomiting together with lethargy, diarrhea, fever, pale or tacky gums, or inability to keep water down
  • Puppies, senior dogs, or dogs with existing conditions — dehydration and low blood sugar come on much faster

One episode, dog seems fine — what to do at home

If your dog is bright and their gums look normal, start by letting the stomach rest. Hold off on food for a few hours, and offer water in small amounts frequently rather than a big bowl at once. Once the vomiting has stopped, offer a bland, low-fat meal such as boiled chicken breast and plain rice in small portions, then transition back to the regular diet gradually over several days. Note that puppies and senior dogs should not be fasted for long — check with your vet before withholding food from them. Never give human stomach medication on your own. Habits that cut down on repeat vomiting

For fast eaters, splitting meals into two or three servings and using a slow-feeder bowl often reduces vomiting noticeably. If empty-stomach bile vomiting is frequent, a small bedtime portion shortens the fasting gap. Scavenging on walks, table scraps, and spoiled treats are all common culprits too. In hot weather, stomach upsets from spoiled food rise, so pay extra attention to how kibble and treats are stored.

My dog threw up once. Do we need to see the vet right away?

If your dog vomited once and is otherwise bright, has normal gum color, and is drinking normally, it is often reasonable to watch them for a few hours. But if the vomiting repeats, contains blood, or your dog is lethargic, painful in the belly, or retching without bringing anything up, go to the vet without waiting. Puppies, senior dogs, and dogs with existing health conditions dehydrate faster, so it is safer to have them seen sooner.

My dog throws up yellow liquid on an empty stomach in the morning. Why?

Yellow vomit is usually bile, and it commonly shows up when the stomach has been empty for a long stretch and bile irritates the stomach lining. If the gap between dinner and breakfast is long, splitting meals into more frequent portions or offering a small bedtime snack often helps. If it keeps happening, or your dog is losing weight or losing appetite, have a veterinarian check for other digestive problems.
